I am a graduate from the University of Iowa with interests in statistical analysis, environmental health, drinking water quality, and the connections between environmental conditions and human health.

My research experience includes constructing an Eddy Covariance flux tower to study prairie restoration and environmental processes related to carbon cycling. Through this work, I gained hands-on experience with environmental instrumentation, data collection, and research supporting climate and land management strategies. My academic background also includes statistics, GIS, and programming in R and Python, which I am interested in applying to data analysis and quantitative research.

I currently work in environmental health, where I test rural private drinking water wells and provide recommendations to property owners. This experience has strengthened my interest in understanding how environmental exposures affect human health and how statistical methods can help identify patterns, evaluate risks, and inform practical solutions.

I am particularly interested in research at the intersection of the environment, statistics, and public health, including drinking water quality, environmental exposures, and the use of data to investigate environmental health outcomes.
